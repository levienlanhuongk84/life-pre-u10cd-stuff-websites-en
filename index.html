After the lesson, students will be able to:

📚Knowledge
  • classify vocabulary related to possessions, consumer habits and website design;

Grammar & sentence patterns

Grammar for this unit

Countable and uncountable nouns · SB p. 122

Source: Mai Lan Huong, p. 5–6

🔢Countable nouns
  • things you can count one by one, in the singular or the plural: a thing, two things
  • use a / an with the singular: a book, an object
  • use numbers, many or a few with the plural: three emails, a few ideas
  • I've already missed three calls since I got home.
  • I also need to reply to a few text messages from work.
🫙Uncountable nouns
  • materials, liquids, abstract ideas and things we see as one mass: stuff, furniture, information, advice, news, happiness
  • no plural -s, no a / an and no numbers before them
  • use some, much or a little before them
  • There's too much stuff everywhere.
  • Can you give me some advice about my website?
⚠️Counting the uncountable
  • count them with a unit word: a piece of information, two bottles of water
  • some nouns change meaning when you count them: two coffees = two cups of coffee
  • Let me give you a piece of advice.
  • Two coffees, please. (= two cups of coffee)

should and could: opinions and suggestions · SB p. 124

Source: Mai Lan Huong, p. 97–106

🔧Form
  • should / could + base verb, with no to after them
  • no -s with he / she / it: she should, not she shoulds
  • negatives and questions need no do: shouldn't + V · Should we + V?
  • I think we should buy it today.
  • You shouldn't spend all your free time online.
  • Should we add a search box?
🎯should: opinion or advice
  • use should to say what you think is the right thing to do
  • should is weaker than must: the listener can still choose to do it or not
  • shouldn't advises someone not to do something
  • We should also have less stuff in our heads.
  • I don't think you should keep that old phone.
💡could: a suggestion
  • use could to offer one possible idea, not a rule: Maybe you could …
  • we / you could … is a suggestion
  • Could you …? is a polite request, not a suggestion
  • could is also the past of can: I could swim when I was five
  • Maybe you could put the text there?
  • We could have a video of the shop on the page.

5-step answer frame

Sample question: "Should people try to own less stuff?"

1Point

Yes, most of us would be happier with fewer things,

2Why

because clutter takes up time and money we could spend on family and hobbies.

3Example

Ryan Nicodemus got rid of his stuff and realized he was much happier.

4Counter

Admittedly, some things have sentimental value and are worth keeping.

5Evaluation

On the whole, though, owning less gives us more space and more time.

Where to use this
  • Part 1: Point + Why (+ Example) — 2–3 clauses.
  • Part 2: follow the cue card's 4 points, each one = Point + Why or Example.
  • Part 2: finish with one Evaluation sentence.
  • Part 3 and the paragraph: use all 5 steps — never skip Counter and Evaluation.
